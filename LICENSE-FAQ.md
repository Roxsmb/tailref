You do **not** need written permission from the copyright holder for any of
these activities. There is no separate commercial license to buy.

## What must I do if I distribute TailRef?

The GPL is a copyleft license. If you distribute TailRef or a modified version
(including as part of a larger distributed work), you must:

1. **Include the license.** Give everyone who receives a copy the complete
   GPL-3.0-or-later text and keep the copyright notices.
2. **Provide the Corresponding Source.** Make the source code for the version
   you distributed available under the GPL. GPLv3 section 6 lists the accepted
   ways to do this, such as shipping the source, including a written offer, or
   providing equivalent access to the source.
3. **Keep it free.** License your modified version under GPL-3.0-or-later. You
   may not add restrictions that contradict the GPL.
4. **Mark changes.** Modified versions must carry prominent notices stating that
   you changed the software and the date of the change (GPLv3 section 5a).
5. **Do not misrepresent the origin.** Do not claim that your modified version
   is the official TailRef release.

If you only use TailRef privately and never distribute it, you do not have to
publish your modifications.

## Can I use TailRef for commercial work?

Yes. Commercial use is permitted. You can use TailRef in a studio, at a company,
for client work, for paid teaching, or in any revenue-generating workflow. The
artwork you create with TailRef is yours; the license covers the software, not
your output.

## Can I sell TailRef or a modified version?

Yes. The GPL permits selling copies, and you may charge for your work,
packaging, support or hosting. If you distribute binaries, the people who
receive them must also receive the Corresponding Source under the GPL. You
cannot use a paid download as a way to avoid the source-code obligation.

## Can I offer TailRef as a hosted service?

GPLv3 does not require you to publish source code merely because users interact
with the software over a network. If you host an unmodified or modified TailRef
and never distribute the software itself, the GPL's source-distribution
obligations are not triggered. If you do distribute the software or binaries to
users, the normal GPL rules apply.

This is different from the AGPL, which TailRef does not use.

## Can I fork the project?

Yes. Forking is allowed under the GPL. If you publish a fork:

- keep the copyright notices and the license text;
- state clearly which changes you made;
- make the fork's source available under GPL-3.0-or-later;
- use a different name or clearly mark the fork as unofficial, and do not use
  the official logo as your fork's logo (see [`TRADEMARK.md`](TRADEMARK.md)).

## Can I make a paid "pro" version or a commercial fork?

Yes, provided the distributed version complies with the GPL. You can charge for
your build, your support, your hosting or your added services. You cannot take
TailRef's code, add restrictions that contradict the GPL, and distribute the
result as a proprietary product. You also cannot present it as the official
TailRef release.

## Do I have to open-source my artwork, brushes or project files?

No. Images, `.tail` projects, brushes, themes and other content you create are
not automatically covered by TailRef's license. Their licensing is up to you.

If an add-on contains TailRef code or is a derivative work of TailRef, the GPL
applies to that part. If it only interacts with TailRef through documented
interfaces and contains no TailRef code, it can have its own license. When in
doubt, ask or treat the combined work as GPL.

## Can I sell brushes, themes, templates or plugins made for TailRef?

Yes. Original assets that you created and that do not contain TailRef code can
be sold under your own terms. If your product includes TailRef code, the GPL
applies to that code and you must provide its source to your recipients.

## What about the name and logo?

Copyright and licensing are about the **code and assets**. Trademarks are about
**names and branding**. The GPL does not give you trademark rights. You may
redistribute the software under the GPL, but you may not present a modified
build as the official TailRef release or use the official logo for your own
product. See [`TRADEMARK.md`](TRADEMARK.md).

The trademark policy does not take away your GPL rights to copy, modify and
redistribute the code.

## What about third-party libraries and fonts?

They are **not** owned by the TailRef author and are **not** relicensed under
the GPL. Each one keeps its own license. The bundled licenses (MIT, BSD,
Apache-2.0, SIL OFL and others) are compatible with distributing them alongside
GPL-3.0-or-later software. See [`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)
for details and review notes.

## If I contribute code, do I lose my copyright?

No. You keep copyright in your contribution. TailRef uses an
"inbound = outbound" policy: by submitting a contribution, you license it to the
project and its users under GPL-3.0-or-later. No CLA and no copyright assignment
are required. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## What happens if I break the terms?

Under GPLv3 section 8, your license terminates if you violate the license. The
license can be reinstated automatically if you cure the violation within 30 days
after receiving notice and it is your first violation; otherwise reinstatement
requires the copyright holder's agreement. There is no separate 32-day cure
period from the old PolyForm license anymore.

## What about older releases?

TailRef 1.4.1 and earlier were published under the PolyForm Noncommercial
License 1.0.0. Copies that were distributed under those terms remain governed by
those terms for those copies. New releases, starting with the first release
after this migration, are licensed under GPL-3.0-or-later. The current source
tree in this repository is GPL-3.0-or-later.
