## Common questions

### Is TailRef open source?

No. It is **free and source-available** under a noncommercial license. The
source is public so people can learn from it, audit it, build on it for
noncommercial purposes, and contribute back. That is different from an
OSI-approved open-source license, which would also allow commercial use.

### Can I fork the project?

Yes, for permitted noncommercial purposes. The license grants the right to make
changes and new works and to distribute them. If you publish a fork:

- keep the copyright notices, the license text and the `Required Notice:` line;
- make clear that your fork is unofficial and not endorsed by the author;
- do not use the TailRef name or logo in a way that suggests it is the official
  project (see [`TRADEMARK.md`](TRADEMARK.md)).

### Can I use TailRef for my freelance or studio work?

Professional work is normally commercial. The license permits *noncommercial*
purposes, so if your use supports a business or paid client work, ask for
written permission first. When in doubt, ask — a short question is cheaper than
a licensing problem.

### Can I use TailRef at my company internally?

Internal use by a for-profit company is generally a commercial purpose even if
you never sell the software itself. Ask for permission before deploying it in a
business environment.

### Can I make tutorials, streams or videos about TailRef?

Talking about TailRef, reviewing it and teaching with it is welcome. If the
video itself is monetized, that is a gray area the license does not spell out.
If monetization is central to what you do, contact the author.

### Can I accept donations or tips?

Voluntary donations for your own time are not the same as selling the software.
The license does not automatically forbid a "buy me a coffee" link. What it does
forbid is charging for the software, for access to it, or for a distribution of
it. Do not use donations as a workaround for paid distribution.

### Can I sell brushes, themes, templates or plugins made for TailRef?

Anything that contains or is built on TailRef code is a derivative work and
falls under the license. Separate original artwork or brush files that you
created yourself are yours; their licensing is up to you. If your add-on
requires distributing TailRef code, ask first.

### What about the name and logo?

Copyright and licensing are about the **code and assets**. Trademarks are about
**names and branding**. The license does not give you trademark rights. See
[`TRADEMARK.md`](TRADEMARK.md).

### What about third-party libraries and fonts?

They are **not** owned by the TailRef author and are **not** covered by the
TailRef license. Each one keeps its own license. See
[`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

### If I contribute code, do I lose my copyright?

No. External contributors keep copyright in their own contributions unless they
sign something that says otherwise. To keep the project able to offer commercial
licenses, contributions that need relicensing require a Contributor License
Agreement. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) and [`docs/CLA.md`](docs/CLA.md).

### What happens if I break the terms?

The license gives you 32 days to fix the problem after written notice. If you
come into full compliance and correct past violations, your license can
continue. Otherwise it ends. See the "Violations" section of the license.

### Is there a warranty?

No. The software is provided "as is", as far as the law allows. Back up your
projects.
