# Code of Conduct

This is the community code of conduct for the TailRef project. It is adapted
from the spirit of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/)
(v2.1), written for a small, independent project. It is not a verbatim copy and
does not claim official adoption by the Contributor Covenant project.

## Our pledge

TailRef is built by an independent developer and used by artists, students and
hobbyists. Everyone who participates — maintainers, contributors, translators,
bug reporters and users — should be able to do so without harassment or
hostility.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful of different skill levels, tools, workflows and opinions.
- Give feedback on the work, not on the person.
- Assume good faith, but accept correction gracefully.
- Keep discussions on topic and specific.
- Respect privacy: do not publish someone else's personal data, private
  messages, artwork or project files without permission.
- Credit other people's work and respect licenses.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking or threats.
- Insults, personal attacks, or repeated hostile comments.
- Discriminatory comments about age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender
  identity or expression, experience level, nationality, personal appearance,
  race, religion or sexual identity and orientation.
- Sexualised language or imagery in project spaces.
- Publishing private information without explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing issues, spamming, or repeatedly ignoring maintainer
  decisions.
- Encouraging or helping others to violate the project license.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, documentation and any chat or email related to the project. It
also applies when someone represents the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a problem to **tailrefstudio@gmail.com**. Include what happened, where,
when and, if possible, links or screenshots. Reports are handled privately.

The maintainer will:

1. acknowledge the report;
2. review the facts and any context;
3. decide on a response;
4. tell the reporter the outcome, as far as privacy allows.

Retaliation against someone who reports a problem in good faith is itself a
violation.

## Enforcement

Responses are proportional to the behaviour and its impact:

1. **Correction** — a private or public note explaining what was wrong and what
   is expected.
2. **Warning** — a formal warning with consequences for repeated behaviour.
3. **Temporary restriction** — a temporary ban from project spaces.
4. **Permanent restriction** — a permanent ban for serious or repeated
   violations.

The maintainer may remove comments, close issues or reject contributions that
violate this code. Decisions are made in good faith and can be appealed once by
email.
