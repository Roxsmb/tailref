# TailRef Trademark Policy

This policy covers the **TailRef** name, the TailRef wordmark, the TailRef logo
and the visual identity of the official project.

It is separate from the software license in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE). The software
is licensed under **GPL-3.0-or-later**; this policy governs names and branding
only. It does not reduce the rights the GPL gives you to copy, modify, build and
redistribute the software.

> **Status:** TailRef is an unregistered name/mark. Nothing in this document
> claims trademark registration, and no registration numbers are implied.

> **TailRef Studio is a brand name, not a separate legal entity.** Copyright in

## Goals

- Let people talk about, review, teach and share TailRef freely.
- Let people fork and redistribute the software under the GPL without confusion
  about what is official.
- Protect users from builds that pretend to be the official release.

## What you may do without asking

- Refer to TailRef by name in articles, videos, tutorials, courses, reviews and
  discussions.
- State truthfully that your project "works with TailRef", "is compatible with
  TailRef", or "is a fork of TailRef".
- Redistribute unmodified or modified versions under the GPL, keeping the
  original copyright and license notices.
- Use the name in a factual, descriptive way (for example in a file name or a
  package description) as long as it is not misleading.

## What you may not do without written permission (branding only)

- Use the TailRef name or logo as the name or logo of your own product,
  service, company or paid offering.
- Suggest that a fork, build, plugin, theme or service is **official**,
  **endorsed**, **sponsored** or **approved** by the TailRef author when it is
  not.
- Use the logo in a way that implies official status, or alter it and present
  the result as the TailRef logo.
- Register domains, social accounts, store listings or package names that
  impersonate the official project.
- Remove or hide the copyright, license or "unofficial" notices from a
  redistribution.

These rules are trademark conditions, not extra restrictions on the software
license. GPLv3 section 7 expressly allows a licensor to decline trademark rights
for some names and marks. If a trademark rule and the GPL appear to conflict
about your rights to the code, the GPL governs the code.

## Forks and third-party distributions

Forks are allowed under the GPL. If you publish one:

1. Keep the original copyright notices and the license text.
2. Make the fork's name clearly different, or clearly mark it as unofficial
   (for example: "TailRef — unofficial fork by …").
3. Do not use the official logo as your fork's logo.
4. Do not present your fork as the place to download the official TailRef.
5. State clearly which changes you made.
6. Make the fork's source available under GPL-3.0-or-later.

## Official repository identity

The official repository is the one published by **Emre Melik Nameli** under the
TailRef Studio brand. Any other distribution should be treated as unofficial
unless the author says otherwise in writing. An unofficial build is still
allowed under the GPL; it simply must not pretend to be the official one.

## Reporting misuse

If you believe someone is misusing the TailRef name or logo, or is presenting an
unofficial build as official, open an issue in the official repository with
links and screenshots.

## Limits of this policy

- This policy does not grant trademark rights and does not claim that any mark
  is registered.
- This policy does not restrict fair, descriptive or nominative use that the law
  allows.
- This policy does not add restrictions to the GPL's grant of rights in the
  software.
- This policy is not legal advice.
